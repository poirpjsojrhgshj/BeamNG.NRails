# Free high-resolution orthoimagery for every US state, DC and territories (research 2026-10-10)

Four regional agents, live-tested 2026-10-10 (one ~150 m tile per source, generic user agent). Test tiles and scripts are in the session scratchpad (us_imagery/, us_scripts/), not in git. Licence lines must be re-verified before release.


---

I covered all 16 states and territories in the region; 48 live fetches gave a real image. **Oregon is the only state with an open statewide service better than NAIP.** Elsewhere the best statewide option is NAIP. Sharper imagery exists only for some cities and counties, and most of those don't state a licence.

Two things decide what "NAIP" means in each row:
- **USDA FPAC "latest NAIP" service (`naip/conus_naip`):** 2024–25 flights, 0.3 m in Montana, Idaho and New Mexico, and about 0.8 s per request. It returned blank images over Denver, Olympia and Honolulu.
- **USGS NAIP service (the one the project plans to use):** older flights (Washington 2017 at 1 m, Colorado 2019), but it covers those two gaps. It took about 10–12 s per request today.

**Top 5 surprises**
1. **Oregon is the best open statewide source in the region** (0.3 m, summer 2024, no stated restrictions). But its TLS certificate expired on 2026-09-26, so a normal HTTPS client fails, and its cache grid isn't standard XYZ.
2. **The two federal NAIP services are stale or patchy.** USGS still serves Washington 2017 at 1 m and Colorado 2019, and took 10–12 s per request today. FPAC's newer imagery (0.3 m in MT, ID, NM) has blanks over Denver and Olympia. Neither covers Alaska, Hawaii or the territories. The server should query both, and the per-state choice matters.
3. **The territories have no working high-res source.** USDA's NRCS host, which carries the 30 cm Guam, CNMI and American Samoa imagery, was unreachable; USDA's older NAIP host (APFO) reset the connection. A third USDA host (FPAC) works, so it's likely those two specific servers rather than all of USDA.
4. **Restrictive licences:**
   - Utah's state service bans scripts.
   - Washington's statewide imagery (WaTech/Hexagon) and Colorado's (Vexcel) are government-only.
   - DRAPP 2024 is behind a login, and LARIAC 2023 is proprietary.
   - King County's EagleView imagery grants no use rights.
   - The Alaska and Hawaii imagery is Maxar-derived (non-commercial at best).
5. **Good city finds with standard XYZ caches:** Maricopa (winter 2025), Reno (3-inch, 2023), Tucson and Sparks (6-inch). None of them states a licence, so treat them as opt-in until the owners confirm. DRAPP 2022 (6-inch, CC BY 3.0) is the only clearly open city source, and only Jefferson County's copy is online.

**Top 5 surprises**
1. **3-inch imagery is common.** Connecticut has statewide CC0 3-inch imagery as a ready-made XYZ cache. Maryland has a 3-inch ImageServer that looks near-statewide and is sharper than the 6-inch layer in the world doc. Rhode Island adds new 3-inch sets twice a year (latest March 2026). DC has 3-inch under CC BY 4.0, and Philadelphia even has a 1-inch 2024 cache.
2. **Two sources have restrictive terms.** NYC's own 2024 tiles fall under NYC.gov terms ("All rights reserved", ask permission), so NYC should come from the NYS service, which has the same source data. West Virginia's leaf-off mosaic is "for viewing only… not redistributed", which leaves West Virginia on NAIP.
3. **Several services are broken or have changed.** All of gis.maine.gov returns 502. NH's 6-inch ImageServer now needs a token; the workaround is MapServer sublayer 102. NYC photo tiles for 2020 and later redirect to an error page. WVGIS dynamic exports time out. NAIP exportImage took about 10 s per request today.
4. **Most of these are standard 3857 XYZ caches** (VT, MA, CT, NJ, PA, DE, VA, NYC, Philadelphia), so they fit the existing XYZ pipeline with no WMS or export wrapper. The exceptions are MD 6-inch (512-px grid), the WV cache (non-standard origin), and the dynamic-only services in NH, NY, RI, MD 3-inch and DC.
5. **Licences are mostly silent rather than open.** Only Connecticut (CC0), DC (CC BY 4.0) and Massachusetts ("No restrictions apply") say anything explicit. Delaware, Vermont, Virginia, New York, Pennsylvania and Philadelphia give only disclaimers. Rhode Island relies on a 2020 email, and its imagery now comes from the vendor EagleView. New Jersey's newest statewide set is still 2020 at 1 ft.
