# Open 3D data as source and benchmark: 3D city models, lidar, photogrammetry, BeamNG scan maps (2026-10-10)

Trigger (user, 2026-10-10): "some places have detailed 3D scans public, some have been made into BeamNG
maps - another reference point".

Scope: (1) open 3D city models with roof shapes, (2) lidar point clouds / DSMs for building heights, roofs and
trees, (3) photogrammetry meshes (Google, Cesium/Bing, open city meshes), (4) BeamNG community maps built from
real scans, (5) how BeamNG.Earth should use each one: as a direct data source, as a benchmark, and in what order.

Markers: **[M]** measured from this machine today (light requests: HEAD, a range GET, one index or one small
API page; generic user agent; nothing bulk-downloaded). **[V-doc]** read in the provider's own docs or terms
page today. **[I]** inference or recommendation. *(verify)* = not confirmed today; re-check before relying on
it. The game and its MCP server were not contacted. No code was changed.

Fits the ROADMAP principles: per-region choices are allowed **for data sources only** ("the best buildings per
country"), never for how the world is built. Every source below feeds the same building/tree pipeline through
one common schema, and the places that have reference data become part of the benchmark set, not special
cases.

## 0. TL;DR

1. **Real roof shapes already exist as open data for a large part of Europe and Japan.** These cover the whole
   Netherlands (3DBAG), about 15 of the 16 German states, all of Switzerland, Estonia, parts of Poland, many
   Japanese cities (PLATEAU) and many single cities.
   - They are CC BY 4.0 or equivalent (dl-de/by, dl-de/zero, swisstopo OGD, PDL 1.0).
   - These licences allow downloading at runtime, caching on the player's disk and converting to game meshes,
     with attribution and a "modified" note.
   - NRW alone: 35,022 tiles of 1 km², 219.6 GB of CityGML, with `roofType` and `measuredHeight` on each
     building [M].
2. **The US has no country-wide LoD2 model, but it has lidar almost everywhere.**
   - USGS 3DEP point clouds are public domain and streamable as EPT on AWS (2,280 projects) [M] or as COPC on
     Planetary Computer [M].
   - Typical US West density is 18–20 pts/m² (LA 2023, San Joaquin 2021, southern Utah 2018) [M].
   - Fells Point is weak: 3.8 pts/m² (2014) and 1.1 pts/m² (2008) in 3DEP [M].
   - The Maryland state lidar server was down for maintenance today [M].
   - US roofs therefore have to be **reconstructed from the lidar** (the 3DBAG method), not downloaded.
3. **France LiDAR HD is the best European lidar for us.**
   - Nationwide, ~10–15 pts/m², 1 km tiles as COPC (range-readable).
   - Tile URLs come from a WFS index. A Paris tile has 14.55 M points in 94 MB, with byte ranges supported [M].
   - Ready-made 0.5 m DSM (MNS), DTM (MNT) and canopy-height (MNH) GeoTIFFs per tile [M].
4. **Do not use Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles or Cesium ion content.**
   - Google forbids pre-fetching, storing or caching beyond `max-age`, offline use, and 3D objects "extracted,
     traced, or otherwise derived ... from Photorealistic 3D Tiles" [V-doc].
   - Cesium ion: "You may not copy, store, or redistribute any portion of Cesium Data Output" [V-doc].
   - Both are incompatible with a disk cache and with game meshes. Humans may look at Google Earth for
     orientation; nothing is captured, traced or automated.
5. **Open city meshes exist but are rare.** Helsinki publishes a CC BY 4.0 reality mesh (OBJ) and textured LoD2
   CityGML [V-doc]. They are useful as a look benchmark; too heavy and too local to stream.
6. **The BeamNG scan maps are calibrated visual references for roads and terrain, not data sources.**
   - Pikes Peak – LIDAR Edition and Road Atlanta – LIDAR Edition by Ewanc (USGS 1 m lidar).
   - Mount Coot-tha by B25Mitch (Geoscience Australia ELVIS lidar).
   - Sangerhausen by ElTacoLoco (German lidar as GLTF).
   - Roane County by bob.blunderton.
   - Drive the same place in our world and in theirs, and compare.
7. **Order of work** [I]:
   - a. Global benchmark harness: footprint IoU, height error, roof-type accuracy, canopy recall, terrain
     residuals, run on reference cities. It works with no new runtime dependency.
   - b. US: roof and height reconstruction from 3DEP lidar for the near ring, which helps Baltimore and the
     whole US West.
   - c. EU: direct LoD2 adapters (3DBAG, NRW, Bavaria, swisstopo), then France LiDAR HD canopy.
   - d. Japan: PLATEAU.

How lidar helps us [I]:
- **Building height and roof shape** per footprint. Classify points as class 6 "building" inside the footprint
  (all listed sets are classified). Fit planes (RANSAC / region growing), take the ridge direction, roof type
  and eave/ridge heights. 3DBAG does exactly this for 10 M buildings, so the method is proven at scale.
- **Trees.** A CHM = DSM − DTM, or the vegetation classes 3–5. Find local maxima for tree tops, take crown
  radius from watershed. LiDAR HD's ready MNH tiles skip the processing.
- **Terrain micro-detail.** A ground-class DTM at 0.5 m for kerbs, ditches, ledges and trail steps near the
  car. Mapterhorn already merges many national lidar DEMs at ~1 m, so the raw cloud is only worth it for the
  physics ring.
- **Cost at speed.** At 134 m/s the car crosses 1 km in 7.5 s. Full-density COPC (94–184 MB/km²) is too heavy
  to fetch on the fly. Use octree levels (EPT/COPC range reads of only the coarse nodes), ready-made
  0.5–1 m DSM/CHM rasters, or derive once per building and cache the compact result (a few bytes per roof)
  forever.

## 5. How BeamNG.Earth should use this

### 5.1 Direct data source (runtime, cached on the player's disk)

One **common building record** for every source [I]:
`{global_id, source, source_version, footprint (lat/lon), base_z, eave_z, ridge_z, roof_type, ridge_azimuth,
roof_planes?(optional, compact), colour?}`.
- Global generator: Overture/OSM footprint + heights + heuristic roof.
- Per-source adapters, which only *override fields*:
  - LoD2 adapters (3DBAG, NRW, Bavaria, Berlin, swisstopo, PLATEAU, Estonia, Poland). These parse the
    CityGML/CityJSON once on the server and keep roof planes simplified to the record.
  - Lidar adapter (3DEP EPT/COPC, LiDAR HD, AHN), which fits roof_type, ridge and heights per footprint from
    class-6 points.
- Meshes are always built by our own generator: stock tileable materials (`m_roof_slates_*`, `*_bat`,
  editor-tools-buildings-veg.md), the roof colour from the de-leaned imagery texel (ROADMAP imagery-lean
  plan), and LODs and merging per 256 m chunk as planned.
- Determinism: the record is keyed by the source's stable id (BAG pand id, AdV `gml:id`, OSM/Overture id).
  `source_version` is pinned in the URL (3DBAG `v20250903`, NRW `timestamp` from index.json). A new source
  version is a new generator input, like a generator-version bump.
- Footprint conflicts: the official LoD2 footprint wins where it overlaps an Overture footprint by IoU > 0.5.
  Otherwise both are kept and reported to the benchmark.
- Payload control:
  - CityGML at 40–56 MB/km² in city centres is too heavy to fetch ahead of a 300 mph car. Prefer the lightest
    form: 3DBAG 3D Tiles glb or API bbox pages, NRW per-km tiles from index.json.
  - Prefetch along the route.
  - Fall back to extrusions when a tile is not ready yet; the roof upgrades later without moving anything,
    since footprint and height stay the same.
  - Cache the compact records (KB/km²), not the raw GML, after parsing.
- Trees:
  - The global source stays Meta/WRI CHMv2 (1.19 m).
  - Where LiDAR HD MNH, AHN or 3DEP exist, the adapter refines tree-top positions and heights.
  - Poland's 3D tree models are a ground truth set.
- Terrain micro-detail: only within the physics ring and only where a 0.5 m lidar DTM is available as a raster
  (LiDAR HD MNT, swissALTI3D via Mapterhorn, EA 1 m). Never fetch raw clouds for terrain alone.

### 5.2 Benchmark (offline tool, reference data never shipped)

### 5.4 Risks
- Each portal changes URLs and versions (3DBAG uses versioned paths; state portals get reorganised). Every adapter needs a health check and a silent fallback to the global generator.
- LoD2 data lags reality by 1–5 years. Overture/OSM may show newer buildings. On conflict, keep the newer
  footprint and take the height from lidar.
- CityGML parsing is CPU-heavy (tens of MB per km²). Parse in a worker, cache compact records, never in the
  frame path.
- Attribution: show the per-source credit when its buildings are on screen (3DBAG requires bottom-right
  placement in map views; our in-game attribution line already follows that pattern).
